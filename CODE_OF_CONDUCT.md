# Code of conduct

Studium is built by teachers, students, researchers and developers. This code says how we treat each other in every project space: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and any event or channel where someone represents the project. It is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), version 2.1.

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Studium to be free of harassment for everyone, whatever their age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation. We act in ways that keep the community open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy.

## What we expect

- Empathy and kindness towards other people.
- Respect for differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences, including those of learners who are new to a field.
- Feedback given and received with care: precise about the work, never about the person.
- Taking responsibility for our mistakes, apologising to those affected, and learning from them.
- Putting what is best for the community, and for learners, ahead of what is best for us.

## What we do not accept

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing someone else's private information, such as a postal or email address, without their explicit permission. This includes learner data of any kind.
- Any other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Responsibilities of the maintainers

The repository maintainers clarify and enforce these standards. They may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that break this code, and they explain the reason when it is appropriate.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces, and when someone officially represents the project in public, for example through an official account or as a named representative at an event.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour privately to the repository maintainers, through GitHub's private reporting on this repository (the **Security** tab, then **Report a vulnerability**; say in the title that it is a conduct report). If that is not available to you, open an issue asking a maintainer for a private contact, with no detail of the incident. Every report is reviewed promptly and fairly. The maintainers respect the privacy and safety of the person who reports.

## Enforcement

The maintainers decide on the consequence of a breach, following these steps:

| Step | When | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Correction | Inappropriate language or other behaviour judged unprofessional or unwelcome | A private written warning that explains the problem; a public apology may be requested |
| Warning | A single serious incident or a series of actions | A warning with consequences for continued behaviour: no interaction with the people involved, including the maintainers, for a stated period |
| Temporary ban | A serious breach, including sustained inappropriate behaviour | A temporary ban from any interaction or public communication with the community |
| Permanent ban | A pattern of breaches, harassment of an individual, or aggression towards or disparagement of groups of people | A permanent ban from any public interaction within the community |
